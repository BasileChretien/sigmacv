# Statutory self-archiving rules — maintainer note

The table lives at `src/lib/archiving/statutoryRights.ts`. It is **data, not scraping**: one entry per country whose law (or national policy) gives an author something towards putting a copy of a publicly funded work in an open repository, keyed by ISO-3166 alpha-2 code, each with the instrument's own name, where it was read, a few short factual phrases and — once a maintainer has read the text — the date it was last checked.

The owner worklist (editor-only) prints one sentence per entry under a closed work whose affiliation on the paper (`meta.workCountries`, from OpenAlex) is in that country. The sentence always says the rule **may also apply**: every one of these rules hangs on conditions no metadata carries — whether the work or the institution was publicly funded, how often the journal appears, whether co-authors agree, the discipline — so whether it applies is the owner's judgement. It sits beside the publisher's policy (OA.Works) and the funder's policy (`docs/FUNDER-OA-POLICIES.md`), never merged into either, and nothing from this table reaches the public page, a public download, the OAI feed or a frozen version (`tests/funders-not-public.test.ts`).

A country whose law gives the author **nothing** is not listed at all: a line saying "no right" would read as a ban under a paper whose publisher may well allow a deposit.

## What each entry carries

- `kind` — `author-right` (a secondary-publication right the author may use: FR, DE, AT, NL, BE, BG), `deposit-requirement` (the law tells the researcher to deposit in a repository: ES — an obligation, and the statements say so), `funding-policy` (no statute; a national policy tied to public grants: JP).
- `sourceUrl` + `sourceKind` — `legal-text` when the page is the text itself (the link reads "legal text"), `policy-text` when it is a government policy document and no statute exists (the link reads "policy text": JP), `guidance` when it is a named page explaining it (the link reads "guidance"). `guidanceUrl` adds a second, explanatory page where one was used.
- `appliesFrom` — the rule's start date, when works from earlier years cannot fall under it: the line is not printed under a work whose year is earlier than that year (a work of the start year, or of no known year, still gets it). Set for BG (2021-06-07: the amending law leaves contracts concluded before 7 June 2021 unaffected, and a work published before 2021 was contracted before then), ES (2022-09-07), JP (2025-04-01, the FY2025 calls), DE (2014-01-01: § 38 Abs. 4 in force since then — Göttingen's FAQ reads it as covering only publications from that date, openaccess.nrw records a contested reading that reaches earlier works with the twelve months counted from 2014-01-01; neither is certain, so the line is not printed under earlier works) and AT (2015-10-01: § 37a in force since then by § 116 Abs. 9 UrhG; the Regierungsvorlage's Erläuterungen say nothing about earlier contracts). Not set for BE (retroactive by law) or FR.
- `workTypes` — the CSL types the rule covers; the line is not printed under any other type. FR, DE, AT, BE and JP cover journal articles (`article-journal`); NL covers the universities' reading of "short works" (`article-journal`, `chapter`, `paper-conference`); BG covers works of scientific literature (`article-journal`, `article`, `chapter`, `book`, `paper-conference`, `report`, `thesis` — not datasets or software); ES and JP carry none.
- `verifiedBy` — `"maintainer"` (the text was read; `lastVerified` is required; the sentence ends "Recorded on _date_") or `"maintainer-pending"` (drafted, not yet confirmed; `lastVerified` is **absent** — the type forbids it and `tests/statutory-rights.test.ts` enforces the pairing; the sentence says "drafted and not yet confirmed against the legal text", never "recorded on").

## Vocabulary

Every entry is tested: the words that would make a sentence read as a verdict or a quantity are never used in an instrument name or a statement — never compliant, compliance, overdue, violation, mandate, mandatory, percent or the percent sign. Write "at least half publicly funded", "asks", "shall", "a requirement". Statements are short (≤ 160 characters), factual and in English; the ten-locale UI wraps them, it does not translate them.

## Candidates deliberately left out

Add each only with a readable source and a `lastVerified` date:

- **Italy** — no secondary-publication right in law: Decreto-legge 91/2013, art. 4 (converted by legge 112/2013) asks public research funders to adopt open-access measures, and a bill creating an author right was pending in 2026. Not listed, because a "no right" line would read as a ban; add an `author-right` entry if the bill passes. (Normattiva renders the text client-side; it was not read automatically.)
- **United Kingdom, United States, Switzerland, …** — no statutory secondary-publication right; funder policies cover them (`docs/FUNDER-OA-POLICIES.md`).

## Adding or updating an entry

1. Find the instrument and the page where its current text is published — the official legal database where it can be read, otherwise a named institutional guidance page that quotes or explains it (and set `sourceKind` accordingly).
2. Write up to eight short statements: who and what is covered, the version, the delay, the conditions (funding, periodicity, co-authors, commercial use), and whether a contract can override it. Omit anything you are not sure of rather than guessing.
3. Set `appliesFrom` when works from earlier years cannot fall under the rule, and `workTypes` when the rule names the kind of publication.
4. Set `lastVerified` to today and `verifiedBy: "maintainer"`; add the country's expected domain (and source kind) to `tests/statutory-rights.test.ts`.
5. Run `npx vitest run tests/statutory-rights.test.ts`.
