# Statutory self-archiving rules — maintainer note

The table lives at `src/lib/archiving/statutoryRights.ts`. It is **data, not scraping**: one entry per country whose law (or national policy) says something about an author putting a copy of a publicly funded article in an open repository, keyed by ISO-3166 alpha-2 code, each with the instrument's own name, where its text lives, a few short factual phrases and — once a maintainer has read the text — the date it was last checked.

The owner worklist (editor-only) prints one sentence per entry under a closed work whose affiliation on the paper (`meta.workCountries`, from OpenAlex) is in that country. The sentence always says the rule **may also apply**: every one of these rights hangs on conditions no metadata carries — how much of the work was publicly funded, how often the journal appears, whether co-authors agree, the discipline — so whether it applies is the owner's judgement. It sits beside the publisher's policy (OA.Works) and the funder's policy (`docs/FUNDER-OA-POLICIES.md`), never merged into either, and nothing from this table reaches the public page, an export, the OAI feed or a frozen version (`tests/funders-not-public.test.ts`).

The sentence depends on `verifiedBy`:

- `"maintainer"` (the text was read; `lastVerified` is required): "…, recorded on _date_".
- `"maintainer-pending"` (drafted, not yet confirmed; `lastVerified` is **absent** — the type forbids it and `tests/statutory-rights.test.ts` enforces the pairing): "…, drafted and not yet confirmed against the legal text". Never "recorded on".

## Kinds

- `author-right` — a secondary-publication right the author may use (FR, DE, AT, NL, BE).
- `deposit-requirement` — the law tells the researcher to deposit (ES). It is an obligation, not an option, and the statements say so.
- `funding-policy` — no statute; a national policy tied to public grants (JP).
- `no-author-right` — the country has no such right in law (IT), stated so the owner does not look for one.

## Vocabulary

Every entry is tested: the words that would make a sentence read as a verdict or a quantity are never used in an instrument name or a statement — never compliant, compliance, overdue, violation, mandate, mandatory, percent or the percent sign. Write "at least half publicly funded", "asks", "shall", "a requirement". Statements are short (≤ 160 characters), factual and in English; the ten-locale UI wraps them, it does not translate them.

## Candidates deliberately left out

Add each only with a readable source and a `lastVerified` date:

- **Bulgaria** — Copyright and Neighbouring Rights Act, art. 60(2)–(4) (2023): publicly funded research, no embargo, non-commercial, contrary agreements void (per the KR21 position paper, 2026-01, annex). The official text (lex.bg) refused automated reading on 2026-09-15 and the KR21 annex is a secondary source, so no URL could be confirmed.
- **United Kingdom, United States, Switzerland, …** — no statutory secondary-publication right; funder policies cover them (`docs/FUNDER-OA-POLICIES.md`).

## Adding or updating an entry

1. Find the instrument and the page where its current text is published — the official legal database where it can be read, otherwise a named institutional guidance page that quotes it.
2. Write up to eight short statements: who and what is covered, the version, the delay, the conditions (funding, periodicity, co-authors, commercial use), and whether a contract can override it. Omit anything you are not sure of rather than guessing.
3. Set `lastVerified` to today and `verifiedBy: "maintainer"`; add the country's expected domain to `SOURCE_DOMAIN` in `tests/statutory-rights.test.ts`.
4. Run `npx vitest run tests/statutory-rights.test.ts`.
